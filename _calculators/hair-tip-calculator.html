How Much Should You Tip a Hairdresser?

20% is the standard for most hair services in the US. That applies whether you're getting a simple trim, a full color, or a blowout. For more complex and time-intensive services like balayage or highlights, tipping 20% to 22% is more appropriate since your stylist is investing significantly more time and skill.

If you're not sure where to start, our general tip calculator works for any service too.

Should You Tip the Salon Owner?

Yes. The old rule about not tipping salon owners because they "set their own prices" is outdated. If someone did a great job on your hair, tip them. Owner or not. Most salon owners today will tell you the same thing if you ask directly.

What About the Shampoo Assistant?

If someone other than your main stylist washed or blow-dried your hair, tip them separately. A $3 to $5 cash tip handed directly to the assistant is the right move. This often gets missed because people tip on the card and assume it gets split — it usually doesn't.

Do You Tip More for Color Services?

For a standard single-process color, 20% is fine. For more complex work like balayage, highlights, or a full color correction, bumping to 20% to 25% is appropriate. These services take longer, require more product, and demand more skill — the tip should reflect that.

For more on tipping at barbershops specifically, check out the barber tipping guide.

Cash vs Card Tips at Salons

Most stylists prefer cash tips. Card tips at salons are often processed through payroll, which means your stylist might wait days or even a week to actually receive it. Cash is immediate and goes directly to them. If you only have a card, still tip — a card tip is always better than nothing.
