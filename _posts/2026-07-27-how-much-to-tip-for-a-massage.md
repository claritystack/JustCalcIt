<h1>How Much to Tip for a Massage?</h1>

<p>You just spent an hour getting all the knots worked out of your shoulders. You feel great. And now you're standing at the front desk with absolutely no idea what to type into the tip screen.</p>

<p>Here's everything you need to know.</p>

<h2>The Standard: 20% for Spa Massages</h2>

<p>At a day spa, resort spa, or massage franchise like Massage Envy or Hand & Stone, 20% is the standard. That's what most therapists expect, and it's what most regulars leave for good service.</p>

<p>15% is acceptable if the massage was average — nothing wrong, just not exceptional. Below that and you're sending a message. Above 20% means your therapist genuinely made your day.</p>

<p>On a $100 massage, that's $20. On an $80 massage, $16. Simple enough.</p>

<h2>The Part Everyone Gets Wrong: Medical Massage</h2>

<p>Here's the distinction most tipping guides gloss over — and it actually matters.</p>

<p>If your massage is happening at a chiropractic clinic, physical therapy office, or medical wellness center — especially if it was prescribed or recommended by a doctor — tipping is generally not expected. The logic is the same as not tipping your doctor or physical therapist. It's a medical service, not a hospitality one.</p>

<p>This trips people up because the actual massage can feel identical. Same table, same techniques, same 60 minutes. But the setting changes the expectation entirely.</p>

<p>Not sure which category you're in? If you booked through your insurance, got a referral, or you're going specifically to treat an injury or chronic condition — you're probably in medical territory and tipping isn't required. If you booked online, paid out of pocket for relaxation, or you're at a place with candles and ambient music — tip 20%.</p>

<h2>Tipping at Different Places</h2>

<p><strong>Day spa:</strong> 20% standard, cash preferred by most therapists since card tips sometimes take days to process through payroll.</p>

<p><strong>Resort or hotel spa:</strong> Check your bill first. Many resorts automatically add a service charge or gratuity — sometimes labeled as "spa fee" or "service charge." If it's already included, you're done. If not, 20%.</p>

<p><strong>Massage Envy, Hand & Stone, or other franchises:</strong> These places explicitly expect tips — they're built into the service model. 20% is standard here. Don't skip it because it feels corporate.</p>

<p><strong>Independent massage therapist:</strong> Same 20% rule applies. Some independent therapists build gratuity into their pricing — if you're not sure, it's fine to ask.</p>

<p><strong>Chiropractor or PT office:</strong> Not expected. Don't feel obligated.</p>

<h2>When to Tip More</h2>

<p>Your therapist spent extra time on a problem area without being asked. They remembered your preferences from last time without you having to explain everything again. They handled a tricky situation — maybe you fell asleep and they quietly kept working — gracefully. The session ran a few minutes long because they weren't done.</p>

<p>In any of those cases, bumping to 25% is a genuine and appreciated gesture. These are skilled professionals, and when someone is exceptionally good at their job, it's worth acknowledging.</p>

<h2>Cash vs Card</h2>

<p>Cash is better — most therapists will tell you this directly if you ask. Card tips at spas often get processed with the therapist's paycheck, which could mean waiting a week or two to actually receive it. Cash is immediate.</p>

<p>That said, card tips are always better than no tip. Don't skip tipping just because you don't have cash on you. Most places make it easy to add a tip at checkout on the card terminal anyway.</p>

<h2>What if You're on a Spa Package?</h2>

<p>If you booked a package with multiple services — massage, facial, nails — tip 15% to 20% on the total package price if you saw multiple providers, or on each service individually if that's easier. The tip gets split among the staff members who worked with you.</p>

<p>Packages at luxury resorts often include gratuity automatically. Always check the booking confirmation or ask at checkout before adding more.</p>
